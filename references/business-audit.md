# The business audit: method

The paid audit most consultants sell first (often called an AI Opportunity Audit): look at how one part of a business runs, find where AI (or a simpler fix) will save time or make money first, and hand over a plain-English report and roadmap. The audit is paid work in its own right, whether or not the client buys the build.

## What you work from

In order of preference:
1. The transcript or notes from the paid working session (where the client showed you how the work happens).
2. Their prep form answers.
3. If there's nothing written down, interview the consultant in two batches of three:

> 1. The business: what do they do, and who for?
> 2. The pain: where does the owner lose the most time, or leak the most money? (The boring, repetitive stuff.)
> 3. What they use now: email, spreadsheets, a CRM, socials, anything else?

> 1. What have they tried already (any AI or automation), and what flopped?
> 2. If one thing got fixed, what would it be?
> 3. Any numbers: hours spent, how often, team size, what an hour of their time is worth? Say "not sure" and the report says so.

Every fact in the report comes from these. If it's missing, it goes in "assumptions" as an estimate, or it's left out.

## What the report says

These map straight onto the report sections in `report-content.md`.

1. **Where things are now:** two or three short paragraphs. How the work flows today in the painful area, said back plainly in their own words, so they feel understood. Name the real bottleneck (often not the thing they asked for).
2. **What's already working:** two or three real things. It builds trust.
3. **How the work happens now (optional map):** the steps as a map. What starts it, the steps, the tools, where it ends up. Mark the bottleneck "Fix first".
4. **Your top opportunities, ranked:** exactly three (two is fine if that's honest). For each:
   - what it is, in one plain sentence
   - what it saves (hours a week or dollars, a real number or a clearly marked estimate)
   - the effort (Quick win, Medium or Bigger build) and a rough time (about a day, about a week, a few weeks)
   - where their existing tools can do it, so they aren't sold a pile of new subscriptions

   Quick wins first, so the client sees momentum. Among the quick wins, the one that fixes the real bottleneck goes first.
5. **What I'd leave alone, and what to be careful with:** what AI isn't the answer for here, or where to go slow (anything that needs a human touch, their brand voice, or a real decision). Include a plain line on keeping their data safe. This section builds more trust than the opportunities do.
6. **Your roadmap:** now, next, later. Keep it to their pace. A nervous owner wants "start small".
7. **The one number we'll track:** one simple metric measured before anything changes (hours on the task, cost per month, response time). Where it sits today. It's what proves the win later, and it becomes the case study.
8. **The numbers, on one page (the ROI one-pager):** the problem in a number, what that costs a year, what fixing it frees up, and the maths shown in one line. One real number beats three pages of explanation.
9. **Next step:** book the 30-minute walkthrough. The pilot gets pitched on that call, not in the report.

## Voice

Written for a non-technical owner who's a bit nervous about AI. Plain English, warm, no jargon. The consultant's name goes on it, so it should read like a capable advisor wrote it. Never "leverage", "synergy", "cutting-edge", "revolutionise" or "unlock potential".
