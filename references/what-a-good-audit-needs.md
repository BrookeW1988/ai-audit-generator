# What a good audit needs

The facts every report depends on. The intake form asks for them, the call fills the gaps, and before the findings you check them again. Each fact has an ID, so you can say "intake answered B4, the call still needs B2".

**Status of each fact:**
- **Have it:** a clear, specific answer (a number, a name, a real example).
- **Vague:** an answer that gives no measure ("heaps", "ages", "a few", "it's a mess"). Treat it as a gap, but quote it.
- **Gap:** not answered.
- **Clash:** two sources disagree (the intake says weekly, the call says daily). Ask about it. Don't pick one.

**Must-haves** (marked ★) are facts the report can't be built well without. If one is still a gap after the call, ask the consultant. If she doesn't know, it goes in "To check" and the report says so plainly.

## Business audit

| ID | The fact | Why the report needs it | Ask on the call if it's a gap or vague |
|---|---|---|---|
| B1 ★ | **The job:** which piece of work, in their words, what starts it, and what "done" looks like | Everything hangs off one clearly named job | "Walk me through [the job] from the very start. What kicks it off, and how do you know it's finished?" |
| B2 ★ | **The people:** who does each step, who signs off, and who would run a new way of doing it | The map, the roadmap and who the pilot is for | "Who touches it, step by step? Who has the final say?" |
| B3 ★ | **How often:** how many a week or month | The numbers page | "Roughly how many of these in a normal week? And a busy one?" |
| B4 ★ | **How long:** time per item or per week, and whether anyone has timed it | The one number to track | "How long does one take, start to finish? Has anyone timed it? Could you time the next one?" |
| B5 ★ | **What goes wrong:** delays, mistakes, redo loops, chasing, complaints | The headline finding | "Where does it get stuck or come back for a redo? Show me the last time that happened." |
| B6 | **Tools:** everything this job touches (and which ones you've seen) | "Uses what you've got" on each opportunity | "Which programs does this go through? Can you show me?" |
| B7 | **What they've tried:** and what happened | Stops you recommending what already failed | "What have you tried already? What happened?" |
| B8 ★ | **Data:** what's sensitive, and which AI tools are OK (or banned) | "What to be careful with", and what's even possible | "Is there anything here you'd never want in an AI tool? Has anyone decided which tools are OK?" |
| B9 | **Human checks:** what a person must always check before it goes out | Where AI stops and a person decides | "What must a person always check before this reaches a customer?" |
| B10 ★ | **What good looks like:** what they'd do with the time, and what would make the audit worth it | The next step and the pitch | "If this was sorted, what would you do with the time?" |
| B11 | **Money:** what an hour of their time is worth, or what the problem costs or loses them | The dollar line on the numbers page (left off if missing) | "What's an hour of your time worth to the business, roughly?" |
| B12 | **The decision:** who decides on next steps, and anything about timing | Who the walkthrough is for | "Besides you, who'd need to say yes to a next step?" |

## Setup audit

| ID | The fact | Why the report needs it | Ask on the call if it's a gap or vague |
|---|---|---|---|
| S1 ★ | **What's built:** each routine, what it's for, and how it's started (typed, scheduled, triggered) | The map | "Walk me through everything you've built. What starts each one?" |
| S2 ★ | **The problem routine:** what it should produce, plus an example of a good result | Area 1 (a clear job) | "Show me a result you were happy with." |
| S3 ★ | **What goes wrong:** stalls, wrong output, doubles, missed items, since when, and how often | The headline finding | "When did it last run all the way through on its own? What changed since then?" |
| S4 ★ | **The rescue:** what they do when it fails, how long that takes, and how often | The one number to track | "What do you do when it stalls, and how long does that take you each time?" |
| S5 ★ | **The load:** how many items per run, and how big | Area 2 (room to think) | "How many does it handle in one go? How long are they?" |
| S6 | **Where and how it runs:** the tool (Claude Code, Cowork, other), the plan, the model, whether the computer needs to be on | Which fixes are possible | "Which plan are you on, and does your computer need to be on for it to run?" |
| S7 ★ | **Data and keys:** client data, email or anything that sends, where keys live | Area 6 (keys and permissions) | "Does it touch client information, or send anything? Where do your API keys live?" |
| S8 | **Who relies on it:** and who else could run it | Area 8 (someone else could look after it) | "If you were away for a week, what would happen?" |
| S9 | **Backups:** git, or any copy of the setup | Area 8 | "If your laptop died tonight, how much of this could you get back?" |
| S10 ★ | **What they want:** fixed for them, or to learn to keep it going, and what they want to build next | The next step, and how you package the fix | "Do you want this fixed, or fixed and taught so you can build the next one yourself?" |
| S11 | **Off-limits:** anything not to touch | The leave-alone list | "Anything you'd rather I didn't touch?" |
| S12 | **Money:** what an hour of their time is worth | The dollar line on the numbers page (left off if missing) | "What's an hour of your time worth, roughly?" |

**Always on screen, never from a form:** for a setup audit, watch the routine run (and fail). For a business audit, watch them do the job. A form can't give you that, and it's where the real finding usually is.
